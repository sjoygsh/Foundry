## 1. What 3D reuses, and what it adds

The earlier architecture was built so that 3D reuses `core`, `platform`, `rhi`, `data`, `asset`
and `scene` without reshaping them (ADR-0003, CLAUDE.md §4). That is the claim this phase tests.
The additions:

| Layer | Addition | Why it sits there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L0 `core` | `Quat`, `Transform` (translation, rotation, scale), `Mat4` inverse and look-at, and the world axes of §2 | Math, like `Vec3` and `Mat4` today; no I/O, no allocation. `physics3d` (L1) and `render3d` (L3) must agree on the axes, and `core` is their only common ancestor |
| L1 `physics3d` | Shapes, queries and a character controller; no dynamics (§8) | Beside `physics2d`, on `core` alone, for ADR-0022's reasons |
| L2 `rhi` | Depth testing, MSAA and resolve, mip chains; later what a pass proves it needs (§4) | The capabilities `rhi.md` §12 left out on purpose |
| L2 `asset` | The runtime mesh, model and material representations (§5); their loaders are `render3d`'s (`meshes.md` §7.4) | Beside the tile grid, which set the precedent: a runtime asset kind lives in `asset`, where the package compiler can check it without linking a renderer |
| L3 `render3d` | Cameras, mesh residency, shading models, lights, passes, culling | Beside `render2d`, on `core`, `rhi` and `asset`: the second renderer API, not a layer over the first |
| L3 `scene` | Engine-declared transform and parent components, and their propagation (§7) | Entities already live here; a hierarchy is how they relate |
| L4 `author` | glTF 2.0 import to Foundry's runtime formats, in the one package compiler that `fpack` and the editor host (`meshes.md` §6) | Build-time interchange, never a runtime format (§5) |

**The two rendering boundaries hold** (CLAUDE.md §4.2). Games and mods see `render3d`, never
the RHI. `render2d` keeps drawing UI and 2D content, over a 3D frame or on its own.

**The projection matrix stays out of `core`.** `core/math.zig` already rules that a projection
baking in one clip space is a landmine for its non-renderer consumers. `render3d` builds its
projection and reads `rhi.clip_space`, as `render2d` does. M19 revises the file's header, which
today says it "does not know which way is up": for 3D it does, because the world axes are a
compatibility decision (ADR-0048) that two layers must share.

## 2. Conventions — locked (ADR-0048)

A 3D convention is seen by every mesh, every animation, every save and every mod. Changing one
later breaks all of them. These hold in `core`, `scene`, `render3d`, cameras, the glTF import,
`physics3d`, animation and the public ABI alike. No subsystem keeps a private variant.

**Space.**
- **Right-handed.** +X is right, **+Y is up**, and **−Z is forward**. +Z points back toward a
  viewer who looks forward.
- **One unit is one metre.** Time is in seconds, as it already is. Mass has no unit yet, because
  nothing has mass (§8).
- **Angles are radians** in code and in the ABI. A content field holding an angle in any other
  unit names that unit in its field name.
- **Forward is −Z for everything that has one:** cameras, lights, characters and the engine's
  forward helper. A camera looks down its local −Z, with +Y up and +X right in its view.

**Matrices.**
- **Column vectors.** A point transforms as `v' = M · v`. `core.Mat4.mul(a, b)` applies `b`
  first, then `a`, as it already documents.
- **Column-major storage,** as `core.Mat4` already has (`cols[c][r]`). Sixteen contiguous
  floats go into a uniform buffer untransposed, for MSL and GLSL alike.
- **A TRS transform composes as `M = T · R · S`:** scale first, then rotate, then translate.
- **The chain to clip space** is `clip = P · V · W · v`: the world matrix `W`, the view matrix
  `V` (the inverse of the camera's world matrix), then the projection `P`.
- **Normals transform by the inverse transpose** of `W`'s upper 3×3. Under uniform scale that
  reduces to `W`'s rotation.

**Rotations.**
- **A rotation is a unit quaternion.** It is stored `(x, y, z, w)` with `w` last, as glTF stores
  it, using Hamilton's convention (`i·j = k`).
- **Positive angles turn counter-clockwise** when viewed looking down the axis toward the
  origin: the right-hand rule. `core.Mat4.rotationZ` already follows it.
- **Rotating a vector** is `v' = q · v · q⁻¹`.
- **Composition matches matrices:** `q = a ⊗ b` applies `b` first, then `a`. The quaternion and
  matrix forms of any chain therefore read in the same order.
- **Stored rotations stay unit.** Every operation that produces a rotation for storage
  renormalises it. A zero, non-finite or badly unnormalised rotation from content, a mod or a
  save is **rejected, not repaired** (I8's graceful refusal).
- **Interpolation takes the shorter arc.** `q` and `−q` are the same rotation, and interpolation
  does not treat them as different.
- **Euler angles are display only,** in an editor or a debug panel. They are never stored,
  serialised or passed across the ABI.

**Local and world.**
- **A `Transform` is always local:** relative to its parent, or to the world for an entity
  with no parent. For a root, local and world are the same.
- **A world transform is derived, never authored and never saved.** It is `W = parent_W · M_local`,
  computed by propagation (§7).
- **A world transform is an affine `Mat4`, not a TRS.** A non-uniform scale under a rotated
  parent produces shear, and a TRS cannot hold shear. Nothing may assume a world matrix
  decomposes back into a `Transform`, and where an operation needs one (§7's re-parent that keeps
  the world pose) it says what it does when the matrix does not decompose.
- **Physics takes rigid poses** (translation and rotation). Scale is applied to a shape's own
  dimensions when the shape is built (§8).

**Geometry and pixels.**
- **Front faces wind counter-clockwise,** as in glTF. Back faces are culled by default. A material
  may be two-sided.
- **Clip space stays `rhi.md` §9's:** x and y in [−1, 1] with +Y up, and depth in [0, 1].
- **Reversed-Z** with a `depth32_float` buffer. The near plane maps to depth 1 and far to 0. The
  depth test passes on greater-or-equal, and depth clears to 0. This spreads precision where
  perspective destroys it, and costs one comparison direction and a clear value.
- **Texture origin top-left, with `v = 0` at the top row** (`rhi.md` §9). glTF's UV origin is
  the same, so import flips nothing.
- **Linear lighting, sRGB storage.** Colour textures are `_srgb` formats. Data textures (normals,
  roughness, masks) are not, and the asset says which. `asset.Image` is sRGB-only today, and M20
  gives it the linear case. Vertex colours are linear, as glTF defines them.

**Import changes nothing silently.** glTF's coordinate system is Foundry's, so the importer copies
positions, rotations and UVs unchanged. glTF's authoring convention is that a *model's* front
faces **+Z**, which is opposite to Foundry's forward. The importer does not guess which assets
mean it:
- geometry keeps its authored coordinates by default, which is what level geometry needs;
- an asset whose front faces +Z says so in its import record, and the importer bakes a half-turn about
  +Y into it, so that its front faces −Z at runtime.

**Large worlds.** `f32` metres resolve about a millimetre at 8 km from the origin, and
everything above is single precision. Coordinates beyond that (a floating origin, or double
precision in simulation) are deferred until a world that size exists (§11).

## 3. Assets are not the renderer — locked (ADR-0053)

A first importer's assumptions are the most likely thing to leak into a permanent API. glTF
nodes, accessors, buffer views and `KHR_` extensions are one file format's shape, not what an
engine needs. So between the source file and the GPU there are **six separate stages**, each
owned by one module, and each translation happens at exactly one seam:

| # | Representation | Owner | What it is | What it never knows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Source files** — `.gltf`, `.glb`, `.png` | the author | Interchange from external tools | — |
| 2 | **Import** | `author`'s package compiler, at build time, hosted by `fpack` and the editor | The only code that reads glTF. It translates source into representations 3–5, and it validates and refuses; it never passes a source concept through | Runtime, GPU |
| 3 | **Runtime mesh** | `asset` | Vertex streams by semantic (position, normal, tangent, UV0, UV1, colour, joints, weights) with their formats declared in the file; indices; submeshes; bounds; later skin data. A versioned binary (I8) | Materials, textures, entities, GPU |
| 4 | **Texture** and **material** | `asset`, and content records | The existing texture kind, extended with mip chains and a colour space. `foundry:material` is a content record: a shading model's ID, its parameters and its texture IDs | Meshes, shader code, GPU |
| 5 | **Model** | `asset`, as a content record | A mesh's parts, each with a submesh, a material slot and a transform relative to the model's origin. A default material per slot, which an instance may override | Entities, glTF nodes, GPU |
| 6 | **Scene** | `scene`, the game | Entities with `foundry:transform`, `foundry:parent` and whatever component a game uses to name a model | Rendering |
| 7 | **Submission** | `render3d`, each frame | Draw a mesh's submesh with a material at a world matrix, from this camera, under these lights | glTF, entities, files |
| 8 | **GPU resources** | `rhi`, owned by `render3d` | Buffers, textures, pipelines and samplers | Everything above |

**The rules that keep the rows apart:**
- **`render3d`'s API names Foundry's runtime types and handles only.** Nothing of glTF's shape
  appears in it: no node, accessor, buffer view, primitive or `KHR_` extension name. A second
  importer (OBJ, FBX, a procedural generator) produces the same rows 3–5, and
  `render3d` cannot tell which one made them.
- **`render3d` does not see entities.** As in 2D, the game or a system extracts what to draw from
  `scene` and submits it. `render3d` sits beside `scene`, not over it.
- **Code can make a runtime mesh too.** A procedural or debug mesh is built as the same in-memory
  representation the loader produces, and it is uploaded by the same path. That is how M19 draws
  before a mesh file format exists (§10).
- **A model is not a hierarchy.** Importing a glTF scene flattens its node tree into a model's
  parts, with each part's transform relative to the model. Whether spawning a model creates
  entities, and so a hierarchy, is `scene`'s decision in M21, not the importer's.
- **Binary payloads stay out of text** (CLAUDE.md §6). A mesh is an asset file, and the model and
  material records reference assets by content ID (ADR-0021).
- **Compressed GPU texture formats** (BC7, ASTC) are a later decision. Their trigger is measured
  memory or load time.

## 4. The RHI widens, it does not change

`rhi.md` §12 kept MSAA, mip generation, texture arrays, cubemaps and compute out until a
consumer existed. M19 proved depth testing and MSAA, M20 mip sampling, and M22 the light
passes below. The types anticipated depth formats, attachments and a `depth_range`.
The work is additive, and
**each capability lands in the milestone whose pass first uses it**, not before:
- **Depth-stencil state** in the pipeline descriptor (test, write, compare), and depth
  attachments (M19). Stencil waits for a pass that uses it.
- **Multisampling** (M19): the sample count on textures and pipelines, and a colour
  attachment's `resolve` target. M19 made resolve a field rather than the store action `rhi.md`
  §8 had shaped the enum for, because Metal and Vulkan both treat storing and resolving as
  independent (`render3d.md` §4.2).
- **Mip chains** (M20): uploading every level, and a view of a level range. `asset` generates the
  levels on the CPU when a texture loads, not the GPU and not the build (`meshes.md` §4.2). The
  trigger to move them to build time is a measured load time, together with compressed formats.
- **M22's light passes:** single-sampled `depth32_float` attachments that may be sampled,
  comparison samplers with declared binding kinds, finite pipeline depth bias, depth-only
  passes, and `rgba16_float` targets blended, resolved at 4× and sampled for tone mapping.
  Cubemaps remain deferred: M22 uses constant ambient, not a sky or image-based lighting.
- **Instancing, texture arrays and compute** wait for a measured trigger: a draw-call count that
  misses a frame budget, shadow cascades that a scene proves it needs, or skinning or culling
  measured too slow on the CPU.

**Every capability lands on all three backends in the same step.** The null backend validates
it, Metal draws it, and Vulkan draws it. That rule made M13 honest, and it is cheaper than a
later port.

## 5. Shaders and materials — locked (ADR-0049)

**Foundry owns its 3D shading models, and writes each one by hand, per backend,** in MSL and in
GLSL compiled to SPIR-V, with the pinned tools ADR-0038 already uses. No shared shader language
or cross-compiler is introduced for neatness. The first set is small:
- **unlit** (M19 with vertex colour, then with a texture in M20);
- **lit** (M22);
- the **skinned** variants of each (M24).

The existing rule is kept: engine-owned shaders are embedded (ADR-0019).

**The abstraction is built so that the shader language can change later without redesigning
the renderer:**
- **A shading model is a registered entry, not a switch statement** (I6). The entry holds:
  - a content ID;
  - the parameter layout the model reads;
  - its texture slots;
  - the pipeline state it requires (blend, cull, depth);
  - its per-backend variant bytes.
- **A material never names shader code.** A `foundry:material` names a shading model's ID and
  sets its parameters and textures. A content mod can therefore make a new material with no code
  (Tier 1), and cannot tell how the model's shaders were produced.
- **`render3d` asks the registry, never a backend's shader language.** Where a variant's bytes
  came from is invisible above the entry.
- **The day mods need to author shaders** (ADR-0015's warning, CLAUDE.md §5), a content-owned
  shader asset kind registers more entries in the same registry. A shared language then changes
  where those bytes come from (cross-compiled, or compiled at runtime from shipped sources), not
  what a material, a submission or `render3d` is.
- **Variants stay few.** Each (model, skinned, alpha mode) pairing is a written variant. A
  permutation generator waits until the hand-written count is a measured burden.

**Transparency follows glTF's three alpha modes:** opaque, mask (alpha-tested) and blend (sorted
back to front).

## 6. `render3d` — forward first (ADR-0052)

**A simple forward renderer**, whose purpose is to establish the architecture and prove meshes,
transforms, cameras, depth, materials, textures, lighting, and both backends. It does not commit
Foundry to deferred, clustered, forward+ or any other advanced design. When a real workload shows
a limit, the renderer changes, based on that measurement.

The API has `render2d`'s shape: begin a frame with a camera, submit, end.
- **Submissions name handles and content IDs, never pointers** (I1, I2). A draw names a mesh, a
  submesh, a material and a world matrix.
- **Sorting and culling happen in `render3d`.** Opaque draws go front to back, and transparent
  ones back to front, with ties broken by submission order, so the result never depends on a
  pointer or a hash (I9). Frustum culling is on the CPU. It is split into jobs by data-determined
  chunks (ADR-0036) only when a measurement asks for that.
- **Passes, in order:** optional directional shadow, world (opaque/mask then transparent),
  Neutral tone map, then `render2d`'s overlay and UI. `render3d.recordFrame` owns the first
  three; `app` owns the overlay. They are fixed. A render graph waits for a consumer that
  needs to reorder passes.
- **Lighting** (M22, ADR-0056): at most sixteen ordered directional, point and spot lights,
  looped over in the forward shader; one shadow-casting directional light; an `rgba16_float`
  target; one Neutral tone map; and the surface's sRGB write. Directional intensity is lux,
  point/spot intensity candela, ambient/emission cd/m². Lit output is pre-exposed by
  `1 / (1.2 · 2^EV100)`; unlit output and the clear colour are not exposed. No named exposure
  means scale 1. Lights are submitted values, not engine records; constant ambient, no IBL.
- **The frame, fences and retirement stay the RHI's rules** (ADR-0035). `render3d` owns mesh and
  material residency the way `render2d` owns its textures.

## 7. The transform hierarchy — locked (ADR-0050)

**The engine declares the transform and hierarchy components.** This is the first
engine-declared component type, and a deliberate exception to M5's rule that the engine owns no
component types: a 3D engine without a shared notion of "where is this thing" pushes every mod
to invent one, which is worse for modding (development rule 12) than one well-chosen name. The
names are permanent public names, and nothing about any particular game is in them.

**The components** (the exact field layout is fixed in M21's design, [`hierarchy.md`](hierarchy.md), within these semantics):
- **`foundry:transform`** holds the local translation (`Vec3`), rotation (unit `Quat`) and scale
  (`Vec3`). It is the only authored pose, and it is saved.
- **`foundry:parent`** holds an entity reference to the parent. It is saved the way `scene`
  already saves entity references inside component data (`scene/save.zig`), so no raw handle
  means anything outside its save (I1).
- **`foundry:world_transform`** holds the derived world matrix. Only propagation writes it; every
  other reader treats it as read-only. It is never saved and never authored. A world whose
  propagation has not yet run for this tick has last tick's value, never garbage.

**Propagation** is one `scene` system at one documented point in the tick: after the systems
that write transforms, and before anything that reads world transforms (collision sync,
animation output and render extraction). It visits parents before children, and entities at the
same depth in ascending entity-handle order, so the result is independent of spawn order and
storage layout (I9). Between propagations a world transform is last propagation's. A system that
needs a freshly computed pose for one entity asks `scene` to compute that entity's chain on
demand, which reads without writing. *As built in M21* (`hierarchy.md` §4.1), that point is
where the host registers `foundry:systems.propagate_transforms`: systems run in registration
order, so the host registers it after its writers, and extraction after `World.update` sees this
tick's poses. Nothing checks the point yet; `before`/`after` system constraints would, and they
wait for the first mod that must insert a system between a writer and propagation.

**Rules:**
- **An entity takes part only if it has `foundry:transform`.** A parent without one contributes
  identity, and that is documented rather than an error.
- **A missing or stale parent** leaves the child a root, reported once. It is never a crash,
  because a save or a mod can name an entity that is gone.
- **Cycles are refused** when the parent is set, by walking the ancestor chain. The depth of a
  chain has a documented limit (`scene.Limits.max_hierarchy_depth`, 64 edges), and exceeding it
  is refused the same way.
- **Re-parenting keeps the local transform by default.** The entity then moves with its new
  parent. A second, explicit call keeps the world pose instead, and refuses when that pose cannot
  be expressed as a `Transform` under the new parent. It never approximates. §7.1 defines it.
- **Despawning a parent despawns its descendants,** deepest first, then in handle order. A child
  meant to survive is detached first. The alternative (orphans that become roots where they
  happen to be) moves things without anyone asking.
- **A 2D game is not required to use any of this.** `samples/sandbox` and `samples/room` keep
  their own components. A world opts in with `World.enableHierarchy()` (M21), and one that does
  not registers none of the three.

### 7.1 Shear, and re-parenting that keeps the world pose

**Shear is legal in a world matrix, and never in a `Transform`.** A `Transform` holds a
translation, a rotation and three scale factors along the rotated axes. Its 3×3 part is always
`R · S`: three orthogonal columns. A world matrix is a product of those, and the product need not
be one. A parent with non-uniform scale, over a child rotated relative to it, gives the child a
world 3×3 of `R_p · S_p · R_c · S_c`, and `S_p · R_c` stretches the child's axes unequally.
- **Example.** A parent scaled (2, 1, 1) with a child turned 45° about Z turns the child's square
  into a rhombus. Its world columns are no longer at right angles.
- **Propagation never decomposes.** It multiplies matrices (`W = parent_W · M_local`), so a
  sheared world is carried down the chain exactly and drawn correctly.
- **Keep-local re-parenting never decomposes either,** so it accepts any valid parent,
  sheared or not.

The only operation that must turn a matrix back into a `Transform` is re-parenting that keeps
the world pose. It is defined exactly as follows. It covers detaching too, where the new
parent's world is the identity.

1. **Validate the request.** The child and the new parent must be live entities with
   `foundry:transform`. The new parent must not be the child or its descendant, and the resulting
   depth must be within the limit. Otherwise it is refused as `NoSuchEntity`, `WouldCycle` or
   `TooDeep`.
2. **Compute both world matrices fresh,** by walking each chain now, not from
   `foundry:world_transform`, which may be a tick old. Call them `W` (the child) and `P` (the new
   parent). Every element must be finite.
3. **Invert the parent.** `P` is refused as `SingularParent` when `|det(P₃ₓ₃)|` is below
   `ε_det · max(1, ‖P₃ₓ₃‖∞)³`, the same tolerance scaled to the matrix's size. Otherwise
   `L = P⁻¹ · W`. It is affine, because both factors are.
4. **Decompose `L` canonically.** Its translation `t` is `L`'s fourth column. The scale is
   `sᵢ = ‖aᵢ‖` over the 3×3's columns `aᵢ`. If `det(L₃ₓ₃) < 0`, the result is a reflection:
   `s_x` is negated, and so is `a_x`, so the rotation is proper. The rotation is the unit
   quaternion of the matrix `[a_x/s_x, a_y/s_y, a_z/s_z]`. Any `sᵢ` that is zero is refused as
   `NotRepresentable`.
5. **Check that the decomposition is exact.** Recompose `L' = T(t) · R(q) · S(s)` by §2's rules.
   **`L` is representable exactly if and only if every element satisfies
   `|L'ᵢⱼ − Lᵢⱼ| ≤ ε_rep · max(1, ‖L‖∞)`**, comparing the 3×3 and the translation alike.
   Otherwise the request is refused as `NotRepresentable`: the columns were not orthogonal,
   which is shear.
6. **Commit, or change nothing.** Only after every check has passed does the call write the
   child's `foundry:parent` and `foundry:transform`, both at once. A refusal at any step writes
   nothing, to the child, the old parent, the new parent or any other entity.
   `foundry:world_transform` is written by the next propagation, never by this call. The error
   says which step refused.

**The tolerances** are `ε_rep = 1e-5` and `ε_det = 1e-6`. That is about eighty-four `f32`
epsilons, room for the dozen operations between `W` and `L'` without admitting visible shear.
They are documented constants, not per-call arguments. M21 may change them only by a recorded
measurement.

**Why refuse, not repair.** Dropping the shear moves the object visibly, which is the silent
approximation this rule exists to forbid. Keeping a sheared matrix as the authored pose would
make `foundry:transform` something other than a TRS, for every consumer. A caller that wants a
nearby representable pose, such as "keep the position and rotation, drop the shear", asks for it
by name: it reads the world matrix, chooses what to keep, and re-parents with keep-local. The
choice is then the caller's, and visible.

## 8. Physics — collision first, no dynamics (ADR-0051)

ADR-0022's argument stands: the deciding issue is I9, not licensing, and Foundry solves the
capability it needs rather than becoming a general physics engine. `physics2d`'s shape carries
over: L1, on `core` alone, with **no time and no velocity**. A caller says *move this by that*,
and is told where it stopped. The game wires it to `scene`, and a body carries an opaque `u64`.

**What M23 builds:**
- **Shapes:** sphere, capsule, box, convex hull and static triangle mesh. The triangle mesh is
  built from a collision asset that the package compiler derives from a mesh or authors separately, like the
  tile grid.
- **Bodies are static or kinematic,** posed by the caller. Nothing has mass, and nothing
  integrates.
- **Filtering:** layers and masks, so a query or a character ignores what it should.
- **Queries:** raycast, shape cast (sphere, capsule, box), overlap, and the closest hit with its
  point, normal and the shape it hit. Triggers are overlap queries that the game asks each tick.
  Nothing calls back into game code.
- **A character controller:** a capsule moved by a desired displacement. It does:
  - collide-and-slide against walls, with a bounded iteration count;
  - ground detection and a ground normal;
  - a maximum walkable slope, steeper than which is a wall;
  - step-up to a configured height;
  - snap-down, to keep contact walking down slopes and stairs;
  - ceiling contact;
  - depenetration out of an initial overlap;
  - no tunnelling at the speeds it is configured for.
  
  Characters block each other and do not push each other. Jump, gravity and movement feel stay
  in the game, which owns velocity.
- **Determinism:** candidates are sorted by handle before use, iteration counts are fixed, and
  there is no fast-math. A replayed input gives the same result on the same machine, and a test
  proves it (I9, ADR-0013).

**Deferred until a game shows the need:** rigid-body dynamics, stacking, constraints and joints,
ragdolls, vehicles, cloth, moving platforms that carry a character, character-to-character
pushing, and heightfield terrain. When dynamics are wanted, Jolt Physics (MIT) is the leading
candidate: it has a documented cross-platform deterministic mode, which answers ADR-0022's
objection in a way Box2D could not. That decision is its own ADR, weighed then against writing a
solver.

## 9. Animation, and what the public ABI gains

- **Skeletal animation** comes from glTF skins and clips, translated into Foundry's own skin and
  clip representations (§3). It is sampled in simulation at the fixed step (I9). Skinning runs on
  the CPU first; GPU skinning is compute's trigger (§4).
- **The public ABI gains 3D in one additive version,** `FoundryApi_v6`. It covers meshes, models
  and materials by content ID; instances; the transform components; cameras; lights; and
  `physics3d` queries and the character. It is published only after the Zig APIs have held
  for a milestone, so mods never see a surface still moving. Every convention in §2 holds across
  it unchanged. The editor stays a client of that table (I4), and a 3D viewport or scene editor
  is its own later milestone.

### 10.1 What the review changed, and why

The order M19 → M26 is sound, and it is kept. Three definitions in it were not:
1. **M19 had no renderer to draw through.** The proposal's M19 drew "a mesh built in code"
   before `render3d` existed (M20). A sample that did so would hold the RHI, which CLAUDE.md
   §4.2 forbids to every game. So M19 gains a `render3d` skeleton and the in-memory runtime mesh,
   and M20 gains the formats and the importer over them.
2. **M19's mesh was "lit" two milestones before light.** Lighting is M22's. M19's mesh is
   unlit, with vertex colour.
3. **M20's draws had no material, although its submissions name one.** Material records were in
   M22. Building `render3d`'s submission path without the material abstraction would shape the
   API around "a mesh and a texture", and M22 would have to retrofit it. So `foundry:material`,
   and the shading-model registry with the unlit model, move to M20, and M22 adds the lit model.

Separately, the RHI's widening is re-timed by consumer (§4). Cubemaps, texture arrays and
instancing leave M19, because nothing in M19 uses them.

**Recorded, not changed:** M25 freezes v6 before M26 plays on it. The playable sample may find a
missing call. That call then goes into an additive later version, as v3–v5 each did. The order
is not reversed, because M26 plays through the Zig APIs, as `samples/room` does, and needs no ABI.

### 10.2 Linux: a reproducible target, not a kept machine

**Linux is a validation target that Foundry can recreate, not a development machine it keeps.**
M18's Linux installation is temporary. Nothing in this roadmap depends on it, or on any
particular Linux machine, staying alive. (It outlived M18: at M19's close the owner booted it
again and asked for the one compile-only change to be confirmed there, which is recorded in
`render3d.md`'s Step 8 Resolution. That was an extra confirmation, not a required run.)
- **M18 establishes the environment.** `linux-desktop.md` records the machine, and
  `scripts/m18/` reproduces it on a fresh Ubuntu x86_64 GPU machine.
  - `provision.sh` installs and hash-checks every pinned tool, and ends by verifying the
    machine.
  - `RUNBOOK.md` gives every command.
  - The user package the runs compile is in the kit (`usercfg/`), not on a machine.
  
  No step relies on state the M18 machine held.
- **M19–M25 run normally on macOS and Windows,** and keep Linux's compile and build coverage.
- **A milestone also runs on real Linux hardware when it materially changes something
  Linux/Vulkan-specific:**
  - window or surface handling on Linux;
  - Vulkan presentation or swapchain behaviour;
  - behaviour that depends on a driver, where Mesa may differ from the Windows driver;
  - any other subsystem whose correctness compiling cannot establish.
  
  **Each milestone's design states its Linux assessment,** "runtime required" or "compile only",
  with the reason, and the milestone's close confirms the assessment still holds. A required run
  provisions a fresh machine with the kit, runs what the change touches, and brings the evidence
  home.
- **No Linux GPU machine runs continuously** to satisfy the roadmap.
- **M26 provisions a fresh Linux GPU environment** and performs the complete runtime and playable
  proof there. **Rebuilding the environment from the kit is itself part of the portability
  proof**: Foundry must work on a newly provisioned system, not only on a hand-configured one.
  Anything the kit was missing is fixed in the repository, not on the machine.
- **Machines are disposable,** cloud instances especially. Before a machine is destroyed, its test
  evidence, logs, documentation and fixes are copied off it and into the repository or the
  record (`RUNBOOK.md` §6). The machine's address, user and credentials never enter either.

## 11. Deliberately not here

The following are out, with no date. Each needs a real 3D game, or a measurement, asking for it:
- **Rendering:** deferred, clustered or forward+ shading; global illumination; ray tracing;
  virtual geometry; occlusion culling beyond the frustum; GPU particles; a render graph;
  cascaded shadows; point-light shadows; compressed texture formats.
- **Large worlds:** LOD, level streaming, terrain and heightfields, procedural world
  construction, and coordinates beyond single precision's metre range.
- **Materials:** an advanced material system or node graphs, mod-authored shader code, and a
  shared shader language.
- **Physics:** dynamics and everything else listed at the end of §8.
- **Everything else:** GPU skinning and compute, D3D12, VR, and a 3D scene editor.

The architecture keeps room for each of these, and none of them is started early:
- §3's separation lets streaming and LOD sit between asset and submission;
- the registry (§5) takes new shading models;
- `physics3d`'s query shape survives a solver beneath it.
