# Design: M41 — Environment: a sky, cubemaps and image-based lighting, light records and generated tangents

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-15, when the owner asked for Step 1, with §14 as written. Steps 1 to 8 are done (the owner played Step 8, 2026-11-16); each step's Resolution follows §15.
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Baseline:** `225fbb9` (ADR-0082 on top of tag `m40`). M0–M40 are complete.
**Paired with:** M45 Input, designed at the same time in [`input.md`](input.md) by a second
session (ADR-0082). See §1.
**Decisions:**
- ADR-0048 (linear lighting, reversed-Z, −Z forward), ADR-0049 (engine shading models per
  backend), ADR-0052 (forward rendering), ADR-0053 (assets are not the renderer), ADR-0055
  (imported models compile to Foundry records), ADR-0056 (photometric units, pre-exposure),
  ADR-0062 (loaders split into a pure decode and a finish) and ADR-0013 (deterministic-friendly)
  constrain it.
- [ADR-0083](../adr/0083-environments-are-content-prefiltered-at-load.md) (proposed): an
  environment is a content record, `foundry:environment`, prefiltered on the CPU when it loads,
  into a specular cubemap with one roughness per level and nine spherical-harmonic coefficients
  for diffuse light. The sky is drawn from the same cube.
- [ADR-0084](../adr/0084-textures-have-layers-and-a-kind.md) (proposed): an RHI texture has
  layers and a kind (`d2`, `d2_array`, `cube`), and a binding declares the kind it expects.
  Cube arrays wait for M87's reflection probes.
- [ADR-0085](../adr/0085-lights-are-records-a-model-places.md) (proposed): `foundry:light` is an
  engine record with `KHR_lights_punctual`'s fields; a model places lights as it places parts;
  glTF lights import field for field. Lights stay values a game submits each frame.
- [ADR-0086](../adr/0086-tangents-are-generated-by-a-mikktspace-port.md) (proposed): tangents
  are generated by Foundry's own Zig port of MikkTSpace in `asset`, checked against the
  reference built in scratch, never by the build.

`light.md` §14 set four of this milestone's items aside: "image-based lighting, a sky and
cubemaps", "an engine `foundry:light` record, and glTF light import", "MikkTSpace tangent
generation", and, with M42, the post-process items. ADR-0060 put the first three here, and
`3d.md` §4, `shadows-cascaded.md` and `shadows-local.md` kept cubemaps and texture arrays for it.

## 1. Purpose and boundary

The roadmap's row is the contract:

> **M41 — Environment.** Sky, cubemaps and image-based lighting; an engine light record, glTF
> light import and tangent generation; also cubemaps and texture arrays in the RHI.

**The runnable result.**
- `sandbox3d`'s yard (M38) stands under a daytime sky. A row of spheres on the hillside runs
  from rough to mirror and from dielectric to metal, and each reflects the sky and the hill's
  colour. Before M41 a metal sphere outdoors reflected one flat constant.
- The room (M22) is lit by its own, dimmer environment rather than the yard's, since one
  environment lights a whole frame (§4).
- A glTF lamp, exported with a light and a normal map but no tangents, imports with its
  light and with generated tangents, and lights the corner it stands in.
- The `dusk` content mod replaces the yard's sky with an evening one, with no code.
- `--environment=off` draws the frame as M40 did, for comparison.
- The court gains its own night sky from its content, and the owner plays it.

**The exit condition:**
- Prefiltering, the spherical harmonics, the cube's face and texel addressing, tangent
  generation and the light record's import agree with CPU oracles.
- The prefiltered bytes of every shipped environment are pinned by hash.
- With no environment, every M22, M24, M39 and M40 readback holds unchanged.
- New Metal readbacks show a mirror sphere reflecting each cube face in the right place, a rough
  one taking a blurred sky, diffuse light matching the harmonics' oracle, and the sky behind
  geometry, at 1× and 4×.
- The yard holds 60 Hz with its environment on macOS/Metal and Windows/Vulkan, and Vulkan
  validation is clean.

**In M41:**
- the RHI's layers, kinds, cube views and per-layer copies (§3);
- tangent generation (§5);
- the light record, model lights and glTF light import (§6);
- the environment record and its prefilter (§7);
- image-based lighting in the lit model (§8);
- the sky pass (§9);
- configuration, statistics and the overlay (§10);
- the samples (§11).

**Not in M41, each owned elsewhere:**
- HDR image sources (Radiance `.hdr`, OpenEXR): M116, "more image formats, including HDR".
  M41's environments are made from ordinary sRGB images scaled by a luminance, or from a
  gradient (§7.1). M116 adds HDR sources to the same record.
- A second tone map, auto-exposure, bloom and colour grading: M42. A sky makes bright pixels
  common, and Neutral already rolls them off; M42 measures it.
- Sky scattering, a sun disc drawn from the directional light, and volumetric fog: M85.
- Reflection probes (local cubemaps, blended per object), screen-space reflections and cube
  arrays: M87. Until then a frame has one environment, and an interior that wants its own
  light uses its own environment.
- Light probes for moving objects: M89; baked lightmaps: M88.
- Prefiltering on the GPU: M75, which brings compute. CPU prefiltering on the background pool
  is measured in §12.
- Compressed cubemap formats: with compressed textures (`3d.md` §3).
- A texture array's first renderer consumer: M43's glyph pages (§3.4).
- Publishing environments and light records to mods: M48 (§12).

**Paired with M45 Input (ADR-0082).** The partner is the earliest later milestone in another
field that shares no engine module with M41 and needs nothing from it either way:
- **M42 Tone and exposure** is passed over: it is the same field, and its bloom and
  auto-exposure read the frames M41 changes in `render3d`.
- **M43 Text I** is passed over: a font's glyph pages are the natural first renderer consumer
  of M41's texture arrays (§3.4), and both change `asset`.
- **M44 Game UI** is passed over: its multi-line editing and layouts need M43's text, and its
  themes are `asset` records.
- **M45 Input** works in `platform`, `app` and `abi`, with `audio` for device change. M41
  changes `rhi`, `asset`, `author` and `render3d`, and no M41 step needs input or audio. M45 is
  the partner.

The two share only documents and the samples' code. The Windows PC is used by one of them at a
time: M41's Step 9 waits for it if M45 holds it.

## 2. What exists

**`engine/src/rhi`:**
- `TextureDesc` (`resource.zig:155`) has a 2D `size`, a format, usage, `mip_levels` and
  `sample_count`. There are no layers, no kind and no view other than the whole texture.
- `BufferToTextureCopy` (`command.zig:169`) names a destination mip level but no layer.
- A bind group layout entry declares `sampled_texture` with `texture = .color` or `.depth`
  (`pipeline.zig:96`). Nothing declares a dimension; every texture is bound as a 2D one.
- Metal creates `MTLTextureType2D` or `2DMultisample`; Vulkan creates `VK_IMAGE_TYPE_2D` with a
  `VIEW_TYPE_2D` view.
- `rhi.md` §12 and `3d.md` §4 listed texture arrays and cubemaps as waiting for a consumer.

**`engine/src/render3d`:**
- `FrameView.ambient` (`renderer.zig:307`) is a constant radiance in cd/m², and
  `lighting.ambient` (`lighting.zig:604`) is its split-sum with Karis's analytic DFG fit,
  mirrored in `lit.metal`'s `ambient_dfg` and the GLSL. "No environment map."
- `FrameUniform` is M40's third layout, 4,256 bytes; `ambient.w` carries the widest penumbra.
- `FrameView.clear_color` clears the HDR target, which is what a game sees behind its geometry.
  The court clears to a dark blue (`court:config.main`'s `clear_linear`).
- The lit shaders already read `tangent` (slot 2, ADR-0054) and apply a normal map. A mesh
  with no tangent stream gets `(1, 0, 0, 1)`.
- `addLight` takes `Light` values (kind, colour, intensity, range, cones, `casts_shadow`,
  `source_radius`, `world`). There is no light record: `sandbox3d:config.main` and
  `court:config.main` keep their lights in their own schemas, and the court's beacons add theirs
  in code.

**`engine/src/asset`:**
- `schemas.zig` declares `foundry:texture`, `foundry:material` v2, `foundry:model` v3 (slots,
  parts, `skeleton`, `clips`, `collision`) and `foundry:model_import` v3.
- `mesh.zig` validates a tangent stream: `float32x4`, unit xyz, w exactly ±1.
- `mesh_builder.zig`'s `Builder` (M28) has positions, normals, UV0, colours and indices. Its
  tangents were left to M41 (`runtime-geometry.md` §3).
- Textures decode on the background pool (`texture_decode.zig`), with mips generated on the CPU
  at load (`mips.zig`), and finish on the main thread (ADR-0062).

**`engine/src/author/gltf/translate.zig`:**
- `normalTexture` without `TANGENT` is refused: "normalTexture needs TANGENT; export tangents"
  (line 272).
- `extensionsRequired` accepts only `KHR_materials_unlit` and
  `KHR_materials_emissive_strength`; any other used extension is a warning, and a node's
  `KHR_lights_punctual` light is ignored.

**The samples:**
- `sandbox3d`'s yard is an outdoor hillside under a clear colour, with constant ambient and the
  sun.
- The room is lit by two lamps, the sun through the window, the spot over the glass and
  constant ambient. The `dusk` mod changes its lights.
- The court is a night scene: a moonlit directional light, constant ambient and the beacons.

## 3. Layers, kinds and cube views (`rhi`, ADR-0084)

### 3.1 The description

`TextureDesc` gains two fields:

```zig
kind: TextureKind = .d2,     // .d2, .d2_array, .cube
layers: u32 = 1,             // .d2: 1; .d2_array: 1..256; .cube: exactly 6
```

A cube is six layers in the order +X, −X, +Y, −Y, +Z, −Z, which Metal, Vulkan and D3D share.
Its faces are square. A multisampled texture is `d2` with one layer. A cube or an array may be
sampled, may be a copy destination and source, and may be a render target one layer at a time
(§3.3). Each limit is refused with `InvalidDescriptor`, and the null backend enforces it.

The kind is fixed for the texture's life, so the backends choose their native type once:
- Metal: `MTLTextureType2DArray` or `TypeCube`.
- Vulkan: `CUBE_COMPATIBLE` on a cube image, `arrayLayers`, and a default view of
  `VIEW_TYPE_2D_ARRAY` or `VIEW_TYPE_CUBE`.

There is no separate view object. ADR-0084 rejects one until a pass needs to read a texture as
two kinds.

### 3.2 Copies

`BufferToTextureCopy` and `TextureToBufferCopy` gain `layer: u32 = 0`. One copy writes or
reads one layer of one level, which is how the renderer uploads a prefiltered cube (§7.4) and
how a test reads a face back.

### 3.3 Rendering into a layer

A colour attachment gains `layer: u32 = 0` beside its mip level. M41 renders into no cube: the
prefilter runs on the CPU. But a readback test of a cube needs every face written by the GPU
and read back, and M87's probes will need it. The attachment field is the whole of that
support, and Step 1 tests it.

### 3.4 Binding

`BindGroupLayoutEntry` gains `dimension: TextureDimension = .d2` (`d2`, `d2_array`, `cube`).
A bind group that puts a texture of another kind in that slot is refused at creation by the
null backend and by the Metal and Vulkan validation paths. A shader declares the same thing in
its own language: `texturecube<float>` in MSL, `samplerCube` in GLSL.

The only renderer consumer of `cube` in M41 is the environment. `d2_array` lands in the same
step for three reasons:
- an array is the same layers and copies with a different view, so it costs one more enum
  value and one more readback test;
- the roadmap names it here;
- M43's glyph pages are its first renderer consumer, and they should find it proven.

§14, item 2 asks the owner to accept that `render3d` uses no array in M41.

**Seamless filtering.** Vulkan filters across cube faces always. Metal filters seamlessly on
every Apple GPU. A test samples a cube at a face's edge and checks that the result blends the
two faces.

## 4. One environment per frame

A frame has at most one environment: `FrameView.environment: ?EnvironmentHandle`, with
`environment_intensity: f32 = 1` and a yaw, `environment_rotation: f32 = 0`. It lights every lit
draw with its diffuse harmonics and its specular cube, and the sky pass draws it behind
everything (§9).

The alternative, an environment per object, is M87's reflection probes, and it needs cube
arrays and a choice per draw. One per frame is enough for the samples:
- the yard is outdoors;
- the room is a separate frame with its own environment;
- the court is one space.

A game moving from outdoors to indoors changes the frame's environment, or blends two by
drawing with each one's intensity. That second idea is not built; M87's blend is the answer.

The constant `FrameView.ambient` stays and is added to the environment's light. A game that
lights only by the environment sets it to zero. That keeps every existing frame unchanged
(`environment = null`), and still lets a game add a fill light under an environment that
leaves a cave too dark.

## 5. Generated tangents (`asset`, ADR-0086)

### 5.1 The algorithm and why it must be MikkTSpace

A normal map is baked against a tangent basis. A renderer that builds a different basis from
the same mesh shows seams and wrong shading where the baker and the renderer disagree.
Blender, Substance and xNormal bake against MikkTSpace, and glTF 2.0 says that a mesh with a
normal map and no tangents is to be given MikkTSpace tangents. So "tangent generation" means
MikkTSpace exactly, not a simpler per-vertex sum.

ADR-0086 makes it **a port to Zig**, in `asset/tangents.zig`, on the same terms as M30's Vorbis
decoder (ADR-0065):
- The reference, `mikktspace.c` (zlib licence), is built in scratch by `zig cc` from a developer
  script, never by the build.
- A test corpus compares every tangent's bits with the reference's.
- The port's licence entry records it as a derived work under zlib's terms.

The port keeps the reference's structure, because its results depend on it:
- welding by position, normal and UV;
- triangles grouped by shared vertex and handedness;
- degenerate triangles handled as the reference handles them;
- the final per-vertex average.

It runs in `f32`, as the reference does, so its bits can match.

### 5.2 Where it is called

- **`asset.mesh.Builder.generateTangents()`** (M28's builder) generates them from positions,
  normals, UV0 and indices that are already set. Runtime geometry gets tangents with no other
  change.
- **`author`'s glTF import** calls the same function for a primitive whose material has a
  `normalTexture` and whose `TANGENT` is absent. Today that is refused; from M41 it is generated,
  and the import's diagnostics say so as a note. A primitive with `TANGENT` keeps its own.
  A primitive with no `TEXCOORD_0` but a normal map is still refused, because there is nothing
  to build a basis from.

Output depends only on the input bytes (ADR-0055), and the generated stream is ordinary
compiled content: a `.fmesh` with a tangent stream, which the runtime cannot tell from an
exported one.

## 6. Light records and model lights (`asset`, `author`, `render3d`, ADR-0085)

### 6.1 `foundry:light`

```
@schema foundry:light (version 1) {
    kind string                          # "directional", "point", "spot"
    color { r f32 g f32 b f32 }          # linear, 0..1
    intensity f32                        # lux for directional; candela for point and spot
    range f32 (default 0)                # metres; 0 unbounded
    inner_cone f32 (default 0)           # radians
    outer_cone f32 (default 0.785398)
    casts_shadow bool (default false)
    source_radius f32 (default 0)        # metres; radians for directional (M40)
}
```

The fields are `render3d.Light`'s except its `world`, and their meaning is
`KHR_lights_punctual`'s with ADR-0056's units. A record has no position, since it describes a
light, not where one is. It validates as `lighting.valid` does, at load, and a record that would
be refused at `addLight` is refused when the content loads, by name.

A record is content, so a mod overrides it by ID (I2, I3). That is the point of the record: a
mod changes a lamp's colour without the game's code, as the `dusk` mod does today only because
`sandbox3d` copies its own schema.

### 6.2 A model places lights

`foundry:model` v4 gains `lights`, a list of `{ light id, translation, rotation }`, each relative
to the model's origin as a part is. Its scale is ignored, because a light has none. The record
stays one of a model's parts in kind: what a model looks like, not what is in a scene.

`render3d.Content.modelLights(model, world, out)` writes each placed light's `Light` value,
with its world matrix composed, into the caller's slice. The game submits them through
`addLight` like any other light, so placement stays the game's and the sixteen-light limit is
unchanged. A model's lights are not drawn by default.

### 6.3 glTF import

`KHR_lights_punctual` joins the imported extensions. For a node with a light, the import:
- writes a `foundry:light` record, `<model>.light<i>`, with ADR-0055's naming;
- adds a `lights` entry to the model at the node's flattened transform;
- copies glTF's `spot.innerConeAngle` and `outerConeAngle`, `range`, `color` and `intensity`
  field for field. ADR-0056 chose glTF's units so that this is a copy.

It refuses:
- an outer cone past π/2;
- a negative intensity;
- a light under a node with non-uniform or negative scale, because a light has no scale and
  the copy would be silently wrong.

`casts_shadow` and `source_radius` are not glTF's. They default to false and zero, and
`foundry:model_import` v4 gains `lights`, a list of `{ name, casts_shadow, source_radius }`
overrides matched by the glTF light's name.

### 6.4 The samples move onto it

`sandbox3d:config.main`'s and `court:config.main`'s `lights` lists become lists of
`{ light id, position, direction }` naming `foundry:light` records. The beacon record names a
light record in place of its own light fields. Each sample's settings reader changes, and its
digests and pins do not, since the values are the same.

This is the engine record's first use, and it means the `dusk` mod overrides records rather
than a sample's list.

## 7. The environment record and its prefilter (`asset`, ADR-0083)

### 7.1 `foundry:environment`

```
@schema foundry:environment (version 1) {
    source string                         # "faces" or "gradient"
    # "faces": six sRGB colour textures, +X -X +Y -Y +Z -Z, square and equal in size
    faces [id] (optional)
    luminance f32 (default 1000)          # cd/m² of a texel of value 1
    # "gradient": three colours, each a linear colour times its luminance (cd/m²)
    zenith { r f32 g f32 b f32 l f32 } (optional)
    horizon { r f32 g f32 b f32 l f32 } (optional)
    ground { r f32 g f32 b f32 l f32 } (optional)
    sharpness f32 (default 4)             # how quickly the horizon meets the zenith
    size u32 (default 128)                # the specular cube's face, 16..512, a power of two
}
```

**Two sources, because M41 has no HDR images.** M116 owns HDR formats.
- **Faces** take six ordinary PNG textures, read in linear light and scaled by `luminance`.
  An author can paint a night sky or photograph a room, but a sun in an sRGB image cannot be
  brighter than white. In the samples, the sun is the directional light, which is what it
  should be anyway in a forward renderer with shadows.
- **The gradient** is three luminances blended by height with an exponent. A daytime sky needs
  nothing else, and its numbers are in content, readable and mod-changeable.

M116 adds `source "image"` with an HDR image. The record's other fields stay.

The sky's luminance is what the sky pass draws, pre-exposed like everything else (ADR-0056).
The yard's daytime zenith is about 6,000 cd/m² at EV100 15, against a sun of about 100,000 lux.

### 7.2 The prefilter, on the CPU, when the record loads

An environment is decoded on the background pool (ADR-0062), as a texture is:
- **the source cube:** the six faces, or the gradient evaluated into six faces of `size`;
- **the specular cube:** `size` down to 8, one level per halving, level *k* prefiltered for
  roughness `k / (levels − 1)`. GGX is importance-sampled with a fixed Hammersley set, 64
  samples on each texel, and each sample reads the source at the mip its solid angle covers
  (filtered importance sampling, Křivánek and Colbert 2008), so a small sample count does not
  alias. Level 0 is the source itself, a mirror. The usual `n = v = r` assumption of the split
  sum is kept, and the existing analytic DFG fit is reused, so no lookup table is added;
- **the diffuse harmonics:** nine coefficients per channel, projected from the source cube by
  each texel's solid angle and convolved with the clamped cosine (Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan 2001).

The result is a value: the cube's bytes as `rgba16_float` and 27 floats. `finish` uploads the
cube, one copy per face per level (§3.2).

Every step is integer-indexed with a fixed order of summation, so the same record makes the
same bytes on every machine (I9, and ADR-0013's not-bit-exact caveat only for the GPU's
sampling). A test pins each shipped environment's bytes by hash.

**The cost, measured in Step 4:** about 6 × (128² + 64² + … + 8²) × 64 samples ≈ 8.4 million
GGX samples for a 128 cube. That should be well under a second on one background worker on
either machine. `size 512` is sixteen times that. §12 records the measured times, and if a
512 cube takes over two seconds, the design note will say so and M75 is the owner of moving it
to the GPU.

### 7.3 Validation

The record is untrusted (I8). The loader refuses:
- a source it does not know;
- faces not six, not square, not equal, or not colour textures;
- gradient colours missing for `gradient`;
- a negative or non-finite luminance;
- `size` out of range or not a power of two.

Each refusal names its field, and none reaches the renderer.

### 7.4 Residency

`render3d.Content` loads an environment by content ID into an `EnvironmentHandle`
(generational, I1), owning its cube texture and its harmonics. It follows the same rules as a
texture:
- it stays resident while a handle holds it;
- a hot reload or a mod override replaces it in place;
- a destroy inside the frame's submission window is deferred (ADR-0063).

## 8. Image-based lighting (`render3d`, the lit model)

**The fourth uniform layout.** `FrameUniform` gains:
- `environment_sh: [9][4]f32`: rgb per coefficient, pre-multiplied by the cosine convolution
  and the frame's intensity;
- `environment: [4]f32`: x the specular cube's top level, y its intensity, zw the rotation's
  cosine and sine.

It grows by 160 bytes, to 4,416. Every shader that declares `Frame` asserts the new size, as M40
did.

**The lit shader.** When `counts.w`'s new bit says an environment is bound:
- **diffuse** is the nine harmonics evaluated at the shading normal, times the existing
  `(1 − dielectric) × base × (1 − metal)` term;
- **specular** samples the cube along the reflection vector, rotated by the environment's yaw,
  at level `roughness × top`, times the existing analytic DFG term;
- both are multiplied by the material's occlusion, as constant ambient is today.

The constant ambient is then added on top (§4). With no environment the shader takes M40's path
exactly, and the cube slot is bound to a one-texel black cube, so the bind group's shape never
varies.

**The oracle.** `lighting.shade` gains an environment argument: the CPU's harmonics and a CPU
cube sampler that reads the same prefiltered bytes, bilinear within a face, at the same level.
The readbacks compare against it within M22's tolerances.

**What it does not do:**
- no horizon or specular occlusion;
- no multiple-scattering energy compensation;
- no parallax correction.

Each is a refinement whose need is measured against the yard. Horizon and specular occlusion
are M81's, beside screen-space ambient occlusion, which supplies the occlusion they need.
Multiple-scattering compensation is M87's with the reflections it brightens. Parallax
correction is also M87's, since it needs a probe's box.

## 9. The sky pass (`render3d`)

After the opaque draws and before the blended ones, a full-screen triangle at reversed-Z depth 0
(the far plane) is drawn with the depth test `greater_equal` and no depth write. It colours only
the pixels nothing opaque covered. Its fragment:
1. rebuilds the view ray from the inverse view-projection;
2. rotates it by the yaw;
3. samples the specular cube at level 0;
4. multiplies by the intensity and the frame's pre-exposure.

That puts the sky in the HDR target before the transparent draws, so glass in front of the sky
blends over it. It draws at the frame's sample count and is resolved and tone-mapped with
everything else.

With no environment, the pass is not recorded, and the clear colour remains what is behind
geometry. One intensity scales both the sky and its light, so the light and its visible source
agree. A game that wants a brighter sky than its light uses a second environment record.

The sky is one draw and one pipeline per backend, `sky.metal` and `sky.vert.glsl` with
`sky.frag.glsl`, engine-owned and embedded (ADR-0019).

## 10. Configuration, statistics and the overlay

- `Config.environment_samples` (64): GGX samples per texel. It is a load-time setting and part
  of the pinned hash's inputs, so the pins name it.
- `Stats` gains `environment` (the frame's content ID, or none) and `sky_drawn`.
- The overlay's render line names the environment.
- `sandbox3d` and the court take `--environment=off`, which submits none, for comparison.

## 11. The samples

**`sandbox3d`:**
- **The yard** gets `sandbox3d:environments.day`, a gradient (a blue zenith, a pale horizon and
  a green-brown ground), and a row of seven spheres on its terrace. Roughness runs 0 to 1 across
  the row, a second row is metal, and their material records live in its content.
- **The room** gets `sandbox3d:environments.room`: six painted faces, generated once by a
  developer script and committed as PNGs, at a dim luminance, so its metal crate edges catch
  the window.
- **The corner** gets a glTF lamp, `sandbox3d:models.lamp`, exported with one
  `KHR_lights_punctual` spot and a normal map without tangents. It is built by a developer
  script from the existing fixtures' tools and committed as `.glb`. The sample places it with
  `modelLights`.
- **The `dusk` mod** overrides `sandbox3d:environments.day` with an evening gradient, and its
  lamps become light-record overrides.
- **The tour** (M38's pins) gains views of the spheres and the lamp. With `--environment=off`,
  every earlier pin holds.

**The court** gets `court:environments.night`: a gradient from a deep blue zenith to a faint
horizon, at a luminance that keeps the court as dark as it is. Its stars, if any, are painted
faces; that is left to Step 8's taste. Its `ambient` falls to a small fill. The owner plays it.

## 12. The public table, platforms and verification

### The public table

  every render target draws into level 0, so `layer` is the only new field.

  per layer, made at creation and retired with the texture. A `d2` keeps its one view.

  resolve target is always a `d2`.
- Texture state is still tracked per texture, not per layer. A barrier moves every layer,
  which is what `render3d`'s uploads will do (§7.4).

## Resolution — Step 2: tangents, a MikkTSpace port (2026-11-15)

**What the step pinned (`asset`, ADR-0086):**
- `asset/tangents.zig`, `tangents.generate(gpa, input)`: positions, normals, UV0 and a
  triangle list in; one `[4]f32` per index out, the tangent and the bitangent's sign as
  `setTSpaceBasic` gives them. It keeps the reference's structure function for function:
  - the grid-hashed weld by position, normal and UV;
  - degenerate triangles moved behind the good ones and given a good corner's space after;
  - triangle info, neighbours from the three-key edge sort, and the four-rule groups;
  - subgroups, and the angle-weighted average at each vertex.
- It refuses by name a stream count that differs, an index count of zero or not a multiple of
  three, an index out of range, more than 2^29 triangles, and a non-finite position, normal or
  UV. The reference's behaviour on non-finite input depends on its compiler, so it is not a
  contract to match.
- **The reference:** `scripts/m41/reference.py` fetches `mikktspace.c` and `.h` at commit
  `3e895b4…`, checks their SHA-256s (also in `AGENTS.md`), and builds
  `scripts/m41/reference/mikktspace_ref.c` with `zig cc -O2 -ffp-contract=off`.
  `make_tangent_corpus.py` writes thirteen meshes and the reference's tangents for each into
  `asset/testdata/tangents/` (388 KB):
  - plain surfaces: a quad, a wavy grid, a cube, a UV sphere with degenerate poles, and a
    48 × 24 torus;
  - mirrored UVs, collapsed UVs (no UV area, so group-with-any), random soup with repeated
    indices and non-manifold edges, and the sphere fully unwelded;
  - degenerate triangles among good ones, an all-degenerate mesh, four triangles on one edge,
    and normals along the UV direction, including a zero normal.
- **Every corpus tangent is bit-equal to the reference's**, which is the exit.
- **`Builder.generateTangents()`** runs it over the builder's positions, normals, UV0 and
  indices, and adds a `float32x4` tangent stream.
- `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/mikktspace.md` records the port as an altered version under zlib's
  terms. The port's header marks it as altered, as clause 2 requires.

**Where the implementation sharpened the design:**
- **Triangles only.** Every Foundry mesh is a triangle list, so the reference's quad paths
  never run, and the port leaves them out:
  - the shorter-diagonal split;
  - `QUAD_ONE_DEGEN_TRI`;
  - the forced quad orientation;
  - `AvgTSpace`, which only a corner shared by a quad's two triangles reaches.

  Also left out are the angular threshold (only the default 180°, whose cosine the reference
  rounds to exactly -1) and the slow fallbacks for a failed `malloc`.
- **The reference is plain IEEE `float` arithmetic.** Clang fuses multiplies and adds into
  FMAs on arm64 by default, and Zig never does, so the tool is built with
  `-ffp-contract=off`. The one double-precision call, `acos`, uses Zig's `std.math.acos` on
  `f64`, and no libm is linked.
- **C's undefined behaviour, pinned:**
  - `(int)` of a NaN grid cell gives cell 0 on every target;
  - the seed's 32-bit shift by 32 is a rotation.

  The sort pivots the seed picks cannot change a result, because every key sorted is distinct
  by the end.
- **One tangent per vertex.** §5.2 did not say how per-corner tangents become a vertex stream,
  so:
  - a vertex keeps its index and its first corner's tangent;
  - each further tangent its corners need becomes one new vertex at the end, a copy of every
    stream, in first-need order;
  - a mesh whose corners agree gains only the stream;
  - a vertex no index names gets +x with a sign of +1;
  - 16-bit indices widen to 32-bit past 65,536 vertices;
  - a split past `Limits.max_vertices` is `TooManyVertices`;
  - a refusal leaves the builder unchanged.
- **A tangent MikkTSpace cannot normalize stays as it is.** A normal along the UV direction, or
  a zero normal, gives a zero vector, and `finish` refuses it as `InvalidTangent`. Step 3
  decides what glTF import does with such a primitive.
- **The sign convention:** on the quad whose +v runs along +z under a +y normal, the reference
  gives -1, as glTF's `w` means: the bitangent is `w · cross(n, t)`.

**Guards verified by mutation.** Each was broken and then restored:
- In the port, an `f32` `acos`, a disabled weld and a disabled degenerate copy each failed the
  corpus test.
- Dropping the index guard crashed two tests.
- In the builder:
  - splitting on agreement instead of disagreement failed four tests;
  - not widening the indices crashed one;
  - dropping the vertex limit failed one.
- Two mutations changed nothing, and that was explained rather than tested:
  - dropping the seed's `+ 3`: the sorts' results do not depend on the pivot;
  - forcing the average: it is unreachable for triangles, so it was removed.

**The bar:** `zig fmt --check`, every `check` (native, Metal, Linux and Windows null), the four
thirty-frame samples and `fdt-check` pass. Tests: null 3,050/3,060 (ten skips), Metal
3,065/3,086 (21 skips); `asset` alone 270/270. No Vulkan, shader or ABI code changed.

## Resolution — Step 3: light records, model lights and glTF import (2026-11-15)

**What the step pinned (`asset`, `author`, `render3d`, ADR-0085 and ADR-0086):**
- **`foundry:light` v1** (`asset/light.zig`) is §6.1's record, registered with the engine's other
  records (`schemas.records`), so `fpack`, the editor and a load all know it. `decode` reads it
  into `asset.light.Light` and refuses, by field:
  - a kind other than directional, point or spot;
  - a colour component outside 0..1;
  - a negative or non-finite intensity or range;
  - cones outside `0 <= inner < outer <= pi/2`;
  - a source radius that is negative, above pi/8 for a directional light, or not inside a
    bounded light's range.

  These are `render3d.lighting.valid`'s rules without the world matrix. The compiler refuses a
  bad record where it is compiled, as it does a particle emitter. An integration test runs both
  rule sets over 15,552 lights and fails if they disagree on any of them.
- **`foundry:model` v4** gains `lights`, a list of `{ light, translation, rotation }`. A v3
  package reads with none.
- **`render3d.Content.modelLights(model, world, out)`** writes each placed light's
  `render3d.Light` at `world · placement` into `out`, and returns the part it filled.
  `lightCount` says how many it needs. The game submits them with `addLight`. Nothing is drawn
  because a model is, and the sixteen-light limit is unchanged.
- **`foundry:model_import` v4** gains `lights`, a list of `{ name, casts_shadow, source_radius }`
  matched by the glTF light's name.
- **glTF import** reads `KHR_lights_punctual`:
  - every light in the file becomes `<model>.light<i>`, by glTF index, placed or not, its
    fields copied;
  - each node that carries a light adds a `lights` entry at its flattened transform, in the
    walk's order;
  - the extension joins the imported set, so a file that requires it imports.
- **Generated tangents.** A lit, unmapped material with a `normalTexture` and no `TANGENT` now
  imports with MikkTSpace tangents, and the import's diagnostics say so as a note. A primitive
  with neither `TANGENT` nor `TEXCOORD_0` is still refused, as §5.2 says.
- **Pins:** the lamp's import output and a mirrored, split tile's are pinned by hash.

**Where the implementation sharpened the design:**
- **Each primitive gets its tangents separately**, because glTF's primitives are separate
  surfaces. Then Step 2's split rule runs over the whole mesh:
  - added vertices go at the end, copies of every stream (joints and weights included);
  - the generated stream holds one tangent per final vertex.

  The rule now lives in `tangents.split`, which `Builder.generateTangents` and the import both
  call, so the two cannot drift. The builder's six tests pass unchanged.
- **A tangent the method cannot normalize is refused at import.** This answers Step 2's open
  case. A zero normal, or one along the UV direction, makes a zero tangent. The import refuses
  it by vertex, naming the fix, instead of compiling a mesh that `finish` or the runtime would
  refuse.
- **A model's broken light is dropped, not the model.** A placement that cannot be read, or
  whose rotation is not a unit quaternion, refuses the model, as a part's does. A light whose
  record is missing, is not a `foundry:light`, or does not decode is dropped and logged, as a
  mesh that fails drops its parts.
- **A model that is not ready places no lights.** A requested model appears whole or not at
  all (`background.md` §8), its lights included.
- **The light record keeps no defaults for `kind`, `color` and `intensity`,** as §6.1 wrote it.
  Every other field defaults to `render3d.Light`'s value. Import always writes all eight fields.
- **glTF's light defaults are applied at import:**
  - colour white, intensity 1, and the spot's inner cone 0 and outer π/4;
  - a written `range` must be positive (glTF's rule), and an absent one is Foundry's 0,
    unbounded;
  - a light under uniform positive scale imports with the scale dropped;
  - one under non-uniform, negative or shearing scale is refused, as §6.3 says.
- **`front "+z"` turns a light as it turns a part:** the half-turn is composed on the right,
  as `meshes.md` does for parts.
- **Limits:** a file's lights and one model's placed lights are capped at 256, which is

- **Not in this step:** the samples moving onto records and the `dusk` mod (§6.4) are Steps 7

## Resolution — Step 4: the environment record and its prefilter (2026-11-16)

`asset/environment.zig` holds `foundry:environment` v1, its decode and validation, and the
prefilter as one pure function, `prefilter(gpa, environment, faces)`. It returns a
`Prefiltered`: the specular cube as `rgba16_float` bytes and nine harmonics a channel. The
schema is registered with the other engine records (`schemas.records`). The compiler checks the
record's meaning as it checks a light's, so `fpack`, the editor and a load refuse alike. It is
not yet loaded by anything; residency is Step 5's.

**Where the implementation corrects or sharpens §7:**
- **The field that picks the source is `kind`, not `source`.** `source` is every asset kind's
  file path (`schemas.source_field`), and the registry reads any record carrying one as a file
  to open. `kind "faces"` or `kind "gradient"` matches `foundry:light`'s spelling.
- **An environment is not a registry asset kind.** It has no bytes of its own, and a `faces`
  environment needs six other assets' pixels. So the prefilter takes decoded `Face` values: an
  image and its texture record's colour space. How Step 5 gathers them and runs the prefilter on
  the background pool is Step 5's to decide; the function is ready for a decode's body.
- **Faces and `size`:** faces must be six equal squares, each `size` times a power of two. They
  are box-filtered in linear light down to `size`, so a face larger than the cube costs one pass
  and no larger buffer. Smaller faces are refused (`FaceSizeInvalid`), because upsampling adds
  nothing. A `linear` texture is refused as `FaceNotColour`.
- **Luminance is capped at 65504 cd/m²,** the largest finite `f16`, both the faces' `luminance`
  and a gradient colour's `l`. The cube stores half floats, and no average or blend of in-range
  texels leaves the range, so the prefilter never clamps.
- **Gradient colours:** `r g b` are refused outside [0, 1], and `sharpness` must be positive and
  finite. The blend toward either pole is `1 − (1 − |y|)^sharpness`, computed with `core`'s
  own `exp2` and `log2`.
- **The source mips are weighted by solid angle,** not a plain 2×2 box. A face's central texels
  subtend about twice its corners'. With a plain box, a bright texel at a face's centre lost about
  half its light at the coarse mips that rough levels read (the roughness-1 test measured 52%).
  Weighted, the lobe's total light is within 2% of GGX's prediction.
- **A bilinear read clamps at a face's edge** and does not blend across into the next face, as
  the GPU's seamless filter would. A rough level reads a coarse mip, where the error is a fraction
  of one texel's weight.
- **The harmonics are pre-convolved:** each band is multiplied by the clamped cosine's weight over
  π (1, 2/3, 1/4). `evaluate(h, n)` therefore returns the radiance a white Lambertian surface
  reflects, and a constant cube evaluates to that constant. The texels' approximate solid angles
  are rescaled to sum to exactly 4π. The basis is the real SH in Ramamoorthi and Hanrahan's
  order, over (x, y, z) in Foundry's world axes.
- **The byte layout:** level by level, largest first; within a level, face by face, +X −X +Y −Y
  +Z −Z; within a face, rows top to bottom. Each face of each level is one RHI copy (§3.2).
  `Prefiltered.faceBytes(level, face)` hands Step 5 each copy's slice.
- **The cost is lower than §7.2 estimated.** Level 0 is copied, not sampled, so a 128 cube takes
  about 2.1 million samples, not 8.4 million.

**The shipped environments.** They are written now, so their bytes can be pinned. Steps 7 and 8
draw them and may tune their numbers, moving the pins on purpose.
- `sandbox3d:environments.day` (the yard) is a gradient.
- `sandbox3d:environments.room` is six 128² faces painted by `scripts/m41/make_room_faces.py`:

## Resolution — Step 5: residency, the fourth uniform and image-based lighting (2026-11-16)

An environment now lights a frame. `render3d.Renderer` owns environments by
`EnvironmentHandle` (I1), `render3d.Content` loads them by content ID, and both lit shaders add
the environment's diffuse harmonics and specular cube to every lit draw. `FrameView` gains
`environment`, `environment_intensity` (0 to 65504) and `environment_rotation` (radians about
+Y). With `environment = null` the frame is M40's: the lanes are zero, the bit is clear, the
black cube is bound, and every M22–M40 readback holds unchanged on Metal.

**Where the implementation corrects or sharpens §7.4 and §8:**
- **Two levels own an environment.** The renderer's is GPU state: `createEnvironment` makes
  an empty one, `writeEnvironment` copies a `Prefiltered` into a new cube texture and its
  harmonics, `destroyEnvironment` is deferred inside the submission window as a material's
  is, and a write there is refused (`InFrame`). A `Prefiltered` whose size, level count, byte
  length or harmonics `prefilter` could not have made is refused (`InvalidEnvironment`) before
  anything is made. `Content` holds the record, its faces and the prefilter, and its handle is
  the renderer's, stable across every rewrite, as a material's is.
- **An empty or pending environment lights nothing.** A frame may name one: the bit stays
  clear and the black cube stays bound. That is how a requested environment waits, and why
  `begin` refuses only a stale or destroyed handle.
- **A rewrite makes a new cube, never writes in place.** A reload may change the size. The old
  cube becomes an orphan and is destroyed once no frame slot's groups bind it: each `prepare`
  rebinds its own slot's world and caster groups to the frame's cube when they bind another, then
  destroys every orphan that no slot binds. No bind group ever names a destroyed texture,
  which Vulkan forbids. The caster group binds the cube too, because a shadowless frame draws
  its world pass with that group (M40).
- **Faces are decoded by a private loader,** `loader.faceLoader`: the PNG on the pool, the
  record's colour space beside it, nothing uploaded. Its registry entries are keyed apart from
  `textureLoader`'s (`acquireWith`), so a face that is also a material texture is decoded once
  for each. Faces up to 4,096 texels on a side are accepted.
- **The prefilter's job copies its faces' pixels,** so the task never reads a payload the
  registry might reload under it. `acquireEnvironment` runs it on the calling thread and returns
  a ready handle, or `InvalidEnvironmentRecord` holding nothing, the refusal said by field or
  face. `requestEnvironment` decodes faces and prefilters on the registry's `core.Tasks`
  (ADR-0083). `environmentState` settles it: it writes a finished prefilter outside the window,
  and starts another when a face's payload has changed since the cube was built, which is how a
  face file's hot reload is followed. The old cube lights until the new one lands. A job whose
  environment is released while it runs is kept until it finishes, then freed.
  `contentChanged` re-reads every environment record and prefilters again as it was obtained.
  A record that no longer reads keeps what it had, as a model's does.
- **`counts.w` is now bits:** `frame_translucent` (1, M40's switch) and `frame_environment` (2).
  Both lit shaders test bit 1 for the transmittance atlas. `FrameUniform` is 4,416 bytes:
  `environment_sh` is at 4,256 and `environment` at 4,400. Every Metal shader that declares
  `Frame` asserts that size, and the SPIR-V agreement check pins both new offsets and bindings 5
  and 6.
- **Binding 5 is a cube, and binding 6 its trilinear clamped sampler,** in the frame group.
  Metal numbers textures and samplers across groups in order, so every material texture and
  sampler moved up by one (3 to 7) in `lit.metal`, `unlit.metal`, `particle.metal` and
  `shadow.metal`. GLSL's set and binding numbers did not move.
- **The yaw:** the environment is turned by `environment_rotation` about +Y, so a world
  direction reads the cube along itself turned back (`lighting.environmentDirection`). The
  normal and the reflection are both turned, and the harmonics are evaluated at the turned
  normal, so no harmonic rotation is needed.
- **The term:** `(harmonics(n) × (1 − dielectric) × base × (1 − metal) + cube(r, roughness × top)
  × intensity × specular DFG) × occlusion`. The harmonics are multiplied by the intensity once,
  in `packEnvironment`, and the oracle multiplies them the same way. The constant ambient is
  added beside it (§4). `lighting.dfg` is now shared by `ambient` and `environmentTerm`, unchanged
  in value.
- **The oracle** is `lighting.shadeLit`, `shade` with an `EnvironmentLight`. `shade` is
  unchanged, so every M22–M40 call stands. It reads the same prefiltered bytes through
  `Prefiltered.sample`: bilinear within a face, clamped at its edge, and linear between the
  two levels either side of the lod. The GPU's seamless filter blends across an edge, so the
  readbacks compare reflections away from a face's edge.

**Readbacks on Metal, at 1× and 4×, within ±2/255 of the oracle:**
- **A mirror plane** reflects each of the six faces of a six-colour cube in place. It is
  untilted, or tilted ±35° about +X to reach ±Y, and yawed a quarter, minus a quarter and a half
  to reach −X, +X and −Z. At three pixels each, the oracle matches, and the face's high channels
  read at least 40 above its low ones. The test caught that a positive turn about +X tips the
  reflection toward −Y.
- **Rough dielectric, rough metal and diffuse planes** under the gradient and the cube are
  tested with a yaw, intensities from 0.7 to 1.5, constant ambient and a directional light.

**Other tests:**
- **Null and Metal:**
  - every write refusal, inside the window and with stale handles, changing nothing;
  - the uniform's lanes and bit only when a written environment is named;
  - an orphaned cube kept until both slots rebind;
  - a destroy in the window deferred past its frame.

## Resolution — Step 6: the sky pass (2026-11-16)

A frame lit by an environment now draws it as its sky. `render3d.Renderer` records one
full-screen triangle at reversed-Z's far plane, tested `greater_equal` against the cleared depth
and writing none, after the opaque and masked draws and before the blended ones. It colours only
what nothing opaque covered, at the frame's sample count, into the HDR target that is resolved
and tone-mapped with everything else. Its fragment reads the cube's top level along the view ray
turned back by the yaw, times the intensity and the pre-exposure, as §9 says. With no
environment, or one that is empty or pending, nothing is recorded and the clear colour stays.

**Where the implementation sharpens §9 and §10:**
- **The sky has its own pipeline layout:** the frame group alone, plus 64 bytes of inline
  constants holding the inverse view-projection. The frame uniform does not carry that inverse,
  and the shading layout would leave the material group unbound, which null refuses (rule 5) and
  Vulkan would have to tolerate. `FrameUniform` is unchanged at 4,416 bytes. The next draw after
  the sky rebinds its own pipeline and groups.
- **The ray is two unprojected points, not the camera's position:** the vertex stage unprojects
  each corner at depths 1 and 0.5, undivided. Both are linear in the screen, so they interpolate
  exactly, and their difference is right for an orthographic camera's parallel rays too.
- **One draw, one pipeline per backend:** `sky.metal`, and `sky.vert.glsl` with
  `sky.frag.glsl`. They are built at `Renderer.init`, embedded (ADR-0019), and checked by two new
  SPIR-V profiles, `sky_vertex` and `sky_fragment`. The vertex profile reads no frame uniform;
  the fragment profile pins every `Frame` offset and bindings 5 and 6.
- **When it is recorded:** exactly when the frame's slot binds a written environment's cube, the
  test the lit shaders' bit already answers. A frame with nothing else to draw still records the
  sky. A view-projection that cannot be inverted draws none; a valid camera's always can.
- **`Stats.environment` is a content ID the renderer is told, not one it looks up.**
  `createEnvironment` takes an optional name, which `Content` sets to the record's ID. A runtime
  environment made without one lights and draws its sky and reports none. The handle stays what
  finds it (ADR-0063). `Stats.sky_drawn` says whether the pass drew. The sky is not counted in
  `draws`, but its pipeline bind is counted in `pipeline_binds`.
- **The overlay's 3D lines** gain `environment content(0x…)  sky drawn`, or `no environment`. The
  overlay has no names for content IDs, so it prints the ID as `core.ContentId` formats it.
- `Config.environment_samples` is Step 4's, unchanged. `--environment=off` is the samples' flag,
  at Steps 7 and 8.

**Readbacks on Metal, at 1× and 4×, within ±2/255 of `skyOracle`** (`Prefiltered.sample` at
level 0, along the turned ray, times intensity and exposure; pixels near a face's edge skipped as
in Step 5):
- **Each of the six faces in place.** The camera looks down −Z. A yaw of a quarter, minus a
  quarter and a half turns it onto +X, −X and +Z, and a pitch of ±1.2 rad onto ±Y. Intensities
  1 and 1.4, and an exposure of −0.5 EV, are covered. At each centre the face's high channels
  read at least 40 above its low ones.

  alone, and unlit glass (alpha 0.5) on the right reads as the premultiplied glass over the sky
  behind it. The open pixels between read as the sky. This holds for the six-colour cube with
  and without a yaw and intensity, and for the gradient at a yaw of 2.
- **Absent:** with a pending environment and with none, the open pixels read as the clear colour,
  and the glass blends over the clear colour.

**On null:** the sky is recorded for a written environment, named or not, and for nothing but
the sky. It is not recorded for none or a pending one. Pipeline binds show it falls between the
opaque and blended draws, the stats name the environment, and no violation is raised.
